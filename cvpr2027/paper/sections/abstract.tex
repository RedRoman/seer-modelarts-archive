\begin{abstract}
A frozen video representation is an appealing source of conditioning for
low-light enhancement, but its usefulness does not establish which parts of
the representation a restoration network needs. We study a fixed
Restormer-style pixel branch conditioned on frozen V-JEPA\,2.1 tokens.
On held-out SDSD sequences, pretrained conditioning improves a matched
zero-token arm by \R{indomain@psnr}\,dB PSNR-Y and
\R{indomain@lpips} LPIPS after a fixed $20{,}000$-step schedule.
The sequence-paired intervals exclude zero and all three training seeds agree
in sign, although effect sizes vary substantially.
A corrected single-seed random-encoder control finds lower LPIPS for pretrained
tokens, while its PSNR-Y difference remains unresolved.
In leave-one-domain-out training, each of three held-out domains improves on
at least one of PSNR-Y and LPIPS without significant degradation of the other.
Inference interventions show that a trained branch tolerates a substantial
reduction of within-clip token variation: projecting centred tokens onto four
principal directions while retaining their full clip mean changes PSNR-Y by
\R{suff@rank4@d}\,dB. A fixed training-set mean and basis perform worse;
this contrast changes both mean and basis. An exploratory validation control
finds contributions from the clip mean and, conditional on that mean,
clip-specific directions. Spatial and temporal averaging have inconclusive effects rather
than demonstrating equivalence.
Hybrid keys modestly improve LPIPS. Spatial grounding does not resolve an improvement, and exposure decoupling
degrades reconstruction under the tested configurations.
Two temporal-loss variants differ in latent directional agreement, while both
worsen perceptual quality and a pixel-space temporal diagnostic.
These results characterize the benefits and limits of one frozen video prior
under a controlled restoration protocol. We release per-sequence measurements
and separate corrected controls from those awaiting reconstruction validation;
none of the projection experiments establishes that the encoder
can be removed.
\end{abstract}
