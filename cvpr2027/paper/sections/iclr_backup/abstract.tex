\begin{abstract}
Low-light video enhancers learn their prior from one paired dataset and degrade on others. We study whether a frozen video world model can supply a better prior. Measuring six frozen encoders on paired dark and well-lit clips, we find that illumination invariance is shared by self-supervised encoders, whereas V-JEPA's latent predictor is unique in forecasting future tokens of dark clips better than copying the last frame. We introduce Seer, which conditions a Restormer-style pixel branch on frozen V-JEPA tokens through residual cross-attention, a learnable cross-layer router and a counterfactual predictor that maps the dark clip's tokens towards those of its well-lit counterpart. On SDSD, Seer is the best of all evaluated methods in fidelity, perceptual quality and motion-compensated temporal consistency. A controlled study that changes only the tokens the pixel branch reads attributes the gain to the pretrained prior: $+\R{deltanovjepa@L0@psnr_rgb}$\,dB on SDSD-indoor over the same network without V-JEPA, not reproduced by a randomly initialised encoder. On unseen datasets the prior does not help on its own. % CROSS-DOMAIN SENTENCE PENDING
Our protocol corrects a frame-pairing error of earlier evaluations and reports the training-set membership of every released checkpoint; all numbers are generated from released per-sequence results.
\end{abstract}
